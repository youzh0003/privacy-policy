1. Why Timeline is no longer on the web

In December 2023 Google announced that "soon your Timeline will be saved right on your device", with optional cloud backups that Google says are automatically encrypted "so no one can read it, including Google" (Google Keyword blog, Dec 12, 2023). The same announcement set auto-delete to three months by default when you first turn the setting on.

Today Google's help pages say:

  • Timeline saves each device's locations to that device (Manage your Google Maps Timeline).
  • "Timeline is not available for Maps on your computer" (Update Google Maps to use Timeline on your device).
  • Backup is optional and stores an encrypted copy on Google's servers for restoration to another device. To enable it, first turn off auto-delete. Changes may take a few days to appear in the backup.

In practice this means your history lives in the Maps app on your phone. If you want a copy you can keep, look at on a bigger screen, or open in another app, you need to export it as a file.

2. Export Timeline on Android, step by step

On Android the export is in your phone's Settings app, not inside Google Maps. According to Google's Android instructions:

  1. Open your phone's Settings app.
  2. Tap Location, then Location services, then Timeline.
  3. Under "Timeline", tap Export Timeline data.
  4. Tap Continue, choose where to save the file (for example Downloads or a cloud drive folder), and tap Save.
  5. Wait for the "Export complete." message.

Menu names vary by phone. Samsung, Xiaomi, OPPO and other makers rename or regroup the Location menu. If you cannot find it, open Settings and search for Timeline.

Google documents the Settings path above. Its Android help page does not specify the export filename. Dawarich's import documentation calls it Timeline.json; filenames alone should not be used to identify the format.

Tips:

  • Export again from time to time. Each export is a snapshot and excludes later activity. If auto-delete is enabled, older Timeline data continues to be removed from your phone.
  • The file contains your precise location history. Treat it like a private document: be careful about which apps and websites you open it in, and about where you store copies.

3. Export Timeline on iPhone

On iPhone and iPad the export is inside the Google Maps app. Per Google's iOS instructions: open Google Maps, tap your profile picture or initial, then Settings → Location & Privacy → Export Timeline data, and save the file with the share sheet (Save to Files). The file is saved as location-history.json.

The iPhone file uses a different layout from the Android file. My Location Timeline does not read iPhone exports yet.

4. Old Takeout files: Semantic Location History vs Records.json

Before the move to on-device storage, many people downloaded their Location History with Google Takeout. If you still have one of those archives (usually a .zip), it may hold two quite different kinds of data:

  • Semantic Location History: Dawarich's import documentation describes monthly JSON files in year folders, with names such as 2013_APRIL.json. My Location Timeline's parser reads place visits and activity segments from this format.
  • Records.json: A separate legacy location-data format documented by Dawarich. My Location Timeline recognizes it but does not currently import it.

Keep the original archive even after you import it somewhere. A Takeout download is a one-time snapshot, and you may not be able to download the same data from Google again.

5. What cannot be recovered

Be careful with anyone who promises to "recover" lost Timeline history. Google's help page says:

  • "When you delete Timeline data, it will be permanently deleted and can't be recovered."
  • If enabled, auto-delete removes Timeline data older than your selected period: 3, 18 or 36 months. You can also turn auto-delete off.
  • "If you factory reset your device or clear Google Play services data, you'll delete Timeline data from your device." Without a backup there is nothing to restore onto a new phone. Moving data to a different device is only described for when backup was on.

No viewer app can bring back data that is not in your phone's Timeline, in your Google backup, or in an export or Takeout file you already saved. If you have none of these, the history is gone.

About My Location Timeline

I built My Location Timeline because I wanted to browse my exported history on the phone itself, without a computer and without uploading it to a website. It is a private, day-by-day location diary that can also record new days going forward, if you allow it (optional; it needs location permission).

  • Formats: imports the Android on-device Timeline JSON and older Google Takeout Semantic Location History monthly JSON files, including those inside a Takeout .zip. iPhone exports and raw Records.json files are not currently supported.
  • Privacy: the file is read on your phone and your history is never uploaded. No app account, no ads, no analytics. Recording, importing and browsing work offline. Buying or restoring Pro uses Google Play.
  • Free: import supported files, see a summary of all imported history, browse the most recent seven days (counted back from today), and preview up to three automatically selected older imported days. For an older archive this means most dates need Pro; the summary and the sample days are free.
  • Retention: imported history is not deleted automatically; only you remove it. Free recordings are kept for 90 days.
  • Pro: Pro is a one-time purchase, with the current local price shown by Google Play. There is no subscription. It unlocks every retained recorded and imported day, keeps your recorded history, and adds GPX/CSV export of recorded days.
