Privacy Policy — Snap to Calendar

Last updated: August 24, 2026

Which app and developer this policy covers

This privacy policy applies to the following Android application and its developer, as listed on Google Play:

  • App name: Snap to Calendar
  • Package name / application ID: com.yzy.snaptocalendar
  • Google Play listing: play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yzy.snaptocalendar
  • Developer name (Google Play developer account): yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore
  • Contact email: yzyapps99@gmail.com

Snap to Calendar ("the app") reads a screenshot, photo or piece of text that you share with it, works out the event it describes, and shows you a card you confirm before it is saved to your calendar or kept as a to-do. This page explains what data the app handles and who it is shared with. The app is provided by yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore ("we", "us").

Summary

  • Your screenshots, photos and text are never uploaded. They are read on your phone, by software running on your phone, and they do not leave it. There is no server of ours for them to go to.
  • The app has no account and no sign-in. We do not know who you are.
  • The events and to-dos you save are stored on your phone. We never receive them.
  • The app does not read your calendar. When you tap "Add to calendar" it hands the event to the calendar app you already use, and that app decides what to do with it.
  • There are no ads, and the app does not collect an advertising ID.
  • The app sends crash reports and a small, fixed set of usage counts so it can be fixed and improved. These carry no screenshot, no photo, no text you shared, and no event details.

What you share with the app

You give the app a screenshot or photo (through Android's photo picker or the share sheet), or plain text (through the share sheet). It then does two things, both on your device:

  • Reads the words in the image. This uses Google's ML Kit text recognition, which is bundled inside the app and runs offline. The image is not sent anywhere.
  • Works out the event. On phones that ship Android's built-in on-device AI (Gemini Nano, provided by the Android AICore system service — Pixel 9 and newer, Galaxy S25 and newer, and a growing list of others), the app asks that system service to interpret the words. AICore is part of the operating system and runs the model on the phone itself; the text is not sent to Google's servers by this app, and we receive nothing. On every other phone the app uses its own built-in date and time detection instead, which is also entirely on the device.

The app has no storage permission and cannot browse your files. It only ever sees the single image you pick in the system photo picker, or the item you send to it from another app.

The image and the text are held only while the card is being prepared, and are discarded when you save or discard the card. The app never keeps a copy of the screenshot.

What is stored on your phone

Everything the app remembers stays on the device:

  • Your history — the title, date, time and place of each event or to-do you saved, and whether a to-do is done.
  • Your monthly capture count — a number and the month it belongs to, so the free allowance can reset.
  • Your Pro purchase status — whether the one-time unlock has been bought.

None of this is sent to us. Uninstalling the app deletes all of it. You can delete any individual history entry from inside the app at any time.

If you have Android's backup switched on, your phone may include the app's history and capture count in the backup Google keeps for your device, under Google's own terms. The Pro purchase record is deliberately excluded from that backup — a restored backup must not carry a purchase onto a device that never made one; Pro is restored from your Google Play account instead.

Your calendar

The app does not ask for calendar permission and cannot read, change or see your calendar. When you tap "Add to calendar", Android opens your own calendar app with the fields already filled in, and you save it there. If you tap "Save as to-do" instead, nothing goes to a calendar at all — the item is kept inside this app, on this phone.

Reminders and notifications

If you save something as a to-do, the app can post a reminder notification. Android asks for your permission at that moment, and not before. Reminders are scheduled and delivered entirely on the device.

Crash reports and usage counts

The app sends:

  • Crash reports — the technical details of a failure, such as the stack trace and the state of the device at the time.
  • Usage counts — a fixed, closed set of events: that a capture started and from which entry point, that extraction finished and whether anything was found, which of the two extraction modes the phone used, that something was saved to a calendar or as a to-do, that the free monthly allowance ran out, and that the purchase screen was shown. Every value is a number, a yes/no, or a label from a fixed list. The app provides no way for the text you shared, an image, an event title, a date or a place to be recorded in these.

This information is tied to a random installation identifier generated by Google's software. We do not ask for or store anything that identifies you personally, but because that identifier is consistent for your installation, this reporting is pseudonymous rather than anonymous. We describe it plainly rather than calling it anonymous.

This version of the app does not offer a switch to turn this off. Uninstalling the app stops all collection.

Advertising

The app contains no advertising and no advertising SDK. It does not collect an advertising ID: the permissions that would let it read one are removed from the app, and the analytics library is configured not to attach one to anything it sends.

Payments

The app is free to install and includes a number of free captures each month. The optional Pro unlock is a single one-time purchase — there is no subscription. It is sold through Google Play. Google processes the payment; we never receive or store your payment details, your card, your name or your billing address. What the app learns from Google Play is whether this Google account owns the unlock. Reinstalling restores it from the same account.

Third-party services

The app uses the following, which handle data under their own policies:

  • ML Kit text recognition — reads the words in an image. Bundled in the app and runs on the device; nothing is uploaded.
  • Android AICore / Gemini Nano — on supported phones, the operating system's own on-device AI interprets the words. It is part of Android, provided by Google, and runs the model on the phone.
  • Google Play Billing — the one-time Pro purchase.
  • Firebase Crashlytics — crash reports (stack traces, device state at the time of a crash).
  • Google Analytics for Firebase — the usage counts described above, plus standard device information such as device model and operating system version. Like any internet service, it also sees the IP address your device connects from, and Google derives an approximate location (typically city or region level) from it. The app has no location permission and cannot read your actual location.

All of the Google services above are operated by Google. See Google's Privacy Policy for how Google handles this data.

Network use

Reading a screenshot and finding the event work with no connection at all. The app uses the network only for the crash reports and usage counts described above, and for Google Play to confirm the Pro purchase.

Data retention and deletion

We hold no copy of your screenshots, your text or your events, so there is nothing on our side to retain or delete. Everything the app stores lives on your phone and is removed when you delete an entry or uninstall the app.

Data collected by the Google services above is retained under Google's retention policies: usage data for a limited period, and crash reports only as long as they are useful for fixing problems. Uninstalling the app stops all collection.

Accuracy

The event details the app suggests are produced by software reading an image, and they can be wrong. That is why the app always shows you an editable card and saves nothing until you confirm it. Every result screen has a report link that opens your own email app with a message already written; it contains no screenshot and nothing quoted from what you shared, and it is not sent until you send it.

Children

The app is a general productivity tool. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes

If our data practices change, we will update this page and the "Last updated" date above before the change takes effect.
