My Location Timeline & History (formerly My Location Timeline; "the app") keeps a private, automatic diary of where you go: it records your location in the background and shows you, for any day, the routes you travelled and the places you stopped. This page explains what data the app handles and who it is shared with. The app is provided by yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore ("we", "us").

Summary

  • Your location history is stored on your phone. The app does not automatically upload your timeline or include it in billing or review requests; files you choose to export may be transferred by your selected storage or sharing service. We do not operate any servers or receive your timeline from the app.
  • The app records location in the background, including when the app is closed — that is its purpose, and it tells you so in a notice before it ever asks for location permission. Recording only runs while you have switched it on, and a persistent notification is shown whenever it is running.
  • No account, no cloud, no ads, no analytics, no crash-reporting software.
  • The app uses Google Play Billing for the one-time Pro purchase and ownership checks, Google Play In-App Review for optional ratings, and Google Play services for location and activity recognition.

Location data

While recording is switched on, the app collects your device's precise location and, through Android's activity-recognition service, the kind of movement detected (such as walking or driving — used to tell journeys apart from stops). From this it builds journeys, visited places, and daily routes.

All of it is written to a database in the app's private storage on your phone. The app does not automatically upload your timeline or include it in billing or review requests. Files you choose to export may be transferred by your selected storage or sharing service. The recording engine runs entirely on the device.

Recording is under your control: it starts only after you tap "Start recording" and accept the background-recording notice, and you can stop it at any time from the app's home screen. While it runs, Android shows the app's ongoing-recording notification and the system location indicator.

Permissions the app asks for

  • Precise location (and background location) — to record your routes. Background ("Allow all the time") access is what lets the timeline keep recording when the app is closed; without it, recording only works while the app has recently been open, and the app says so honestly rather than pretending otherwise.
  • Physical activity — to detect automatically when a journey starts and ends.
  • Notifications — the persistent "recording is on" notification, and nothing else. Notifications never contain your location.
  • The app also uses standard system capabilities to keep recording reliable: a foreground service marked as location use, permission to resume after your phone restarts, and a wake lock so recordings are saved correctly.

Storage, retention and deletion

Everything lives in the app's private storage on your device:

  • Free version: a rolling 90 days of history is kept on the device and older records are deleted automatically; the calendar lets you view the most recent 7 days.
  • Timeline Pro (one-time purchase): your full history is kept for as long as you like, and you can export it.
  • If Pro stops being active on your Google account (for example after a refund), history older than the free window is not deleted immediately: the app warns you first and gives you a grace period of at least 30 days, during which you can export that history for free before it is removed.
  • Your timeline is excluded from Android device backups, so it is not copied into your Google account backup. Moving to a new phone means using the app's export.
  • Uninstalling the app permanently deletes your entire timeline stored in the app. Files you exported are not deleted.

Importing a Google Maps Timeline export

If you choose to import a Google Maps Timeline export, the app reads that file on your phone and stores the result on your phone. The app does not upload the file or its contents as part of importing.

  • The app reads the Timeline export from Android's Timeline settings (Timeline.json) and older Google Takeout location history: a Semantic Location History month file (for example 2019_JANUARY.json) or the Takeout .zip archive. A .zip is read in memory, file by file; nothing from it is unpacked to storage. Only Timeline files inside it are imported. Every other file in the archive is read past and not stored.
  • You choose the file yourself, either with Android's system file picker or by sharing it to the app from another app (for example a Files app). Either way the app gets read access to that one file only, adds no new permissions, does not keep a copy of the file, and always shows what it will do before it reads anything.
  • Older Google Takeout files carry no time zone, so the app asks which time zone to show them in before importing. That choice is stored with the imported entries on your phone.
  • The imported places and journeys are stored in a separate database in the app's private storage on your device. Where the export includes them, this also holds place names and addresses. The database is excluded from Android device backups.
  • Imported history is never deleted automatically. The 90-day rule above applies only to days the app recorded itself. Without Pro, you can see a summary of the whole import, your most recent 7 days and up to three older sample days; with Pro, every imported day is open.
  • You can delete one import, or all imported history, at any time in the app's Settings, under Imported history. Deleting one import also deletes the place names it brought in. Deleting is always free. Uninstalling the app also deletes it permanently.
  • Each import diagnostic entry contains the logging timestamp, elapsed time since boot, a local batch ID, format, counts, duration and result, but no file name, place names, addresses, coordinates or dates from the imported timeline.

The app is not affiliated with Google.

Guide: How to export your Google Maps Timeline on Android

Export

With Pro, you can export your timeline as a GPX or CSV file. You choose where the file is saved using Android's system file picker; the app writes the file there and keeps no other copy. An exported file contains your location history in readable form — the app reminds you of this before every export, and how you store or share that file is up to you.

Network use

Our own app code makes no direct network requests. Google Play Billing and its bundled dependencies can communicate with Google, and the Billing dependency chain adds the INTERNET permission. Google's handling of data is subject to Google's Privacy Policy. Google Play In-App Review added no permission: the app asks the Google Play Store app on your phone to show its rating card, and if you post a rating or review, Google Play sends it, not the app. The app does not automatically upload your timeline or include it in billing or review requests; files you choose to export may be transferred by your selected storage or sharing service.

Purchases

The one-time Timeline Pro unlock is sold through Google Play. Google processes the payment and we never receive or store your payment details. Whether you own the unlock is stored on your device and re-checked with Google Play, so it survives reinstalling the app.

No ads, no analytics, no crash reporting

The app contains no advertising SDK, no analytics SDK and no crash-reporting SDK, and it does not collect an advertising ID. We receive no usage data of any kind from the app. Your device's own system-level reporting to Google (such as Android's crash and diagnostics settings) is separate from the app and governed by your device settings and Google's privacy policy.

Third-party services

The app uses the following Google services:

  • Google Play Billing — handles the one-time Pro purchase and ownership checks. Data is processed by Google Play under Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Google Play In-App Review — offers optional ratings. Automatic prompts are enabled only in release builds, after at least four counted opens across at least three distinct days and seven days since installation; returning after at least 30 minutes in the background counts as a new open. A prompt may follow the first departure from an import result that added a place or journey, or opening an unlocked past day containing a recorded stop or journey, subject to screen and timing checks. The app normally waits at least 60 days after a review flow that may have displayed the card. Google Play decides whether the card actually appears. Any rating or review you submit goes to Google Play under your Google account and is handled under Google's Privacy Policy. Reviews for a published release are public on Google Play; feedback for a closed test is shared privately with the developer. The app sends nothing about your timeline with the request and is never told whether you rated it, what rating you gave or what you wrote. "Rate this app" in Settings simply opens the app's Google Play page. After a review flow completes successfully or reaches the app's 90-second wait limit, the app attempts to write one local diagnostic entry containing the logging timestamp, elapsed time since boot, the event name "review_asked", and the counted opens and distinct days. That log stays on your phone unless you save it to a file yourself from Settings.
  • Google Play services — provides location and activity recognition, subject to Google's Privacy Policy.

Data retention and deletion requests

We do not hold any of your data ourselves, so there is nothing for us to delete on request — the app stores your timeline on your phone, where you can delete it by uninstalling the app (imported history can also be deleted in the app's Settings). Purchase records are held by Google Play under Google's retention policies.
