Privacy Policy — Summarize PDFs and Photos

Last updated: August 17, 2026

Which app and developer this policy covers

This privacy policy applies to the following Android application and its developer, as listed on Google Play:

  • App name: Summarize PDFs and Photos
  • Package name / application ID: com.yzy.docsummary
  • Google Play listing: play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yzy.docsummary
  • Developer name (Google Play developer account): yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore
  • Contact email: yzyapps99@gmail.com

Summarize PDFs and Photos ("the app") reads a document you choose and returns a short summary, a list of key points and a few extracted fields, each pointing back to the page it came from. This page explains what data the app handles and who it is shared with. The app is provided by yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore ("we", "us").

Summary

  • This app sends the text of the document you choose to an AI service so that it can be summarised. That is what the app does, and it cannot work without doing it. Please read "Your documents" below before you use it on something sensitive.
  • The file itself never leaves your phone. The app extracts the text on your device and sends only that text — never the file, never its name, never where it is stored.
  • We do not keep the text. It is held in memory only for as long as the request takes, and it is not written to any database or log of ours.
  • No account is needed and the app never asks you for personal details. It signs in anonymously in the background so that we can count your free monthly credits.
  • There are no ads, and the app does not collect an advertising ID.
  • The app sends crash reports and basic usage counts so it can be fixed and improved. These are tied to a random installation identifier, so they are not anonymous, and they never include your documents, their names or their contents. This version of the app has no switch to turn them off; uninstalling stops all collection.

Your documents

When you choose a document, the app opens it on your device and extracts the text, page by page. It then sends that text to our server, which passes it to an AI service that produces the summary. The result comes back to your phone and is shown to you.

What is sent is only the page text and the number of pages. The request the app makes has no field for a file name, no field for a file location, and no field for the file's bytes, so there is nothing else about the document for it to send — by the shape of the request, not by us remembering to leave it out.

What we do with it:

  • We do not store it. Our server holds the text in memory for the length of the request and then discards it. It is never written to a database, a file, or a backup.
  • We do not log it. Our server's logs record categories, counts and timings — never document text, never excerpts, never the values the AI extracted.
  • We do not read it. No person at our end sees your document. Nothing in our systems is designed to let us retrieve it, because nothing keeps it.
  • We do not use it to train anything. Neither we nor our AI provider train models on it (see below).

The file you choose is never modified, moved or copied out of its place. The app has no storage permission and cannot browse your files: it only ever sees the one document you pick in the system file picker, or that you send to it from another app.

The AI service we use

The summarising is done by OpenAI, acting as our service provider. The page text is sent to OpenAI's API from our server, together with the instructions that tell it what kind of summary to produce.

  • Every request we make sets OpenAI's store option to false, which instructs OpenAI not to retain the request or its response for later retrieval.
  • API call logging is switched off on our OpenAI organisation account, so prompts and responses are not kept in our account's logs.
  • OpenAI does not use data submitted through its API to train its models.
  • OpenAI may hold a copy briefly for its own security and abuse monitoring before deleting it. That is OpenAI's process, not ours, and we cannot switch it off on your behalf.

See OpenAI's Privacy Policy and its API data-usage policies for how OpenAI handles data it receives.

Please think before you send

The app is useful precisely on documents that matter — a tenancy agreement, a bill, a letter from an insurer. Because the text of whatever you choose is sent off your device to be read by an AI service, you should treat this app the way you would treat pasting the same text into any online service, and avoid using it on material you are not permitted to disclose to a third party, such as someone else's confidential or medical records.

Your anonymous account

The app signs in anonymously through Firebase Authentication the first time it runs. This creates a random identifier for your installation. It is not linked to your name, email address, phone number or Google account, and we never ask you for any of those.

Against that identifier we keep a small record on our server containing only counters and timestamps: how many credits you have used this month, how many free credits remain, when the allowance resets, and counters used to stop automated abuse. It contains no document text, no file names, and nothing that identifies you.

If you uninstall the app or clear its data, your device forgets that identity and the app creates a new one the next time it runs. The old record is left holding nothing but counters.

Crash reports and usage counts

The app sends:

  • Crash reports — the technical details of a failure, such as the stack trace and the state of the device at the time.
  • Usage counts — a fixed, closed set of events: that the app was opened and how, that an analysis started, finished or failed, how many pages the document had, how many credits were used, how long it took, and, if something failed, which of the app's own error categories applied. The app provides no way for a file name, a document's text or an extracted value to be recorded in these — every value is a number, a yes/no, or a label from a fixed list.

This information is tied to a random installation identifier generated by Google's software. We do not ask for or store anything that identifies you personally, but because that identifier is consistent for your installation, this reporting is pseudonymous rather than anonymous. We describe it plainly rather than calling it anonymous.

This version of the app does not offer a switch to turn this off. Uninstalling the app stops all collection.

Reporting a summary

Every result has a "Report this summary" action. It opens your own email app with a message already written, addressed to us. That message identifies the request and the kind of document only — it does not contain the document, the summary, or anything quoted from either, and it is not sent until you send it. You are welcome to add your own description of what went wrong; there is no need to attach or quote the document, because we have no copy of it and do not want one.

Network use

The app needs network access to work at all: to sign in anonymously, to send the document text for summarising and receive the result, and to send the crash reports and usage counts described above. Unlike a purely on-device tool, it cannot function offline.

Abuse prevention

To stop the free allowance being drained by automated traffic, the app uses Firebase App Check with the Google Play Integrity API. This asks Google to confirm that the request is coming from a genuine, unmodified copy of this app on a genuine Android device. It reports on the app and the device's integrity, not on you, and it does not give us your identity.

Payments

This version of the app is free and sells nothing. It gives a number of free credits each month. If a paid plan is added later, it will be sold through Google Play, Google will process the payment, we will never receive or store your payment details, and this page will be updated before that change takes effect.

Advertising

The app contains no advertising and no advertising SDK, and does not collect an advertising ID.

Third-party services

The app and its server use the following services, which handle data under their own privacy policies:

  • OpenAI — summarising the page text, as described above.
  • Firebase Authentication — the anonymous sign-in described above.
  • Cloud Firestore — the credit counters described above.
  • Firebase App Check / Google Play Integrity — abuse prevention.
  • Firebase Crashlytics — crash reports (stack traces, device state at the time of a crash).
  • Google Analytics for Firebase — the usage counts described above, plus standard device information such as device model and operating system version. Like any internet service, it also sees the IP address your device connects from, and Google derives an approximate location (typically city or region level) from it. The app itself has no location permission and cannot read your actual location.

All of the Firebase and Google services above are operated by Google. See Google's Privacy Policy for how Google handles this data.

Data retention and deletion

Your document text is not retained by us at all, so there is nothing of it to delete. Our servers pass it on, return the answer, and keep no copy.

The credit record described under "Your anonymous account" is kept for as long as the app is in use, because it is what stops the monthly allowance from being reset by reinstalling. It holds counters only.

Data collected by the Google services above is retained under Google's retention policies: usage data for a limited period, and crash reports only as long as they are useful for fixing problems. Uninstalling the app stops all collection.

If you want the record associated with your installation deleted, write to yzyapps99@gmail.com and we will delete it. Because the record is deliberately not linked to any personal detail, we may need you to help us identify which one is yours.

Children

The app is intended for people aged 13 and over, and says so when it first runs. It is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Summaries are produced by an AI model, which can be wrong; the app says so on the result itself.
