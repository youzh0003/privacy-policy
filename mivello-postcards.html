Privacy Policy — Mivello Postcards

Last updated: September 18, 2026

Which app and developer this policy covers

This privacy policy applies to the following Android application and its developer, as listed on Google Play:

  • App name: Mivello Postcards (listed on Google Play as "Mivello Postcards: Pet Travel"; the same app is listed as 《Mivello 風寄小箋：小獸旅行》 in Traditional Chinese and 《Mivello 风寄小笺：小兽旅行》 in Simplified Chinese, and its launcher icon reads simply Mivello)
  • Package name / application ID: com.yzy.farawaypost (the package keeps an older internal spelling, "farawaypost"; it is an identity, not a name, and it is the same app)
  • Google Play listing: play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yzy.farawaypost
  • Developer name (Google Play developer account): yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore
  • Contact email: yzyapps99@gmail.com

Mivello Postcards ("the app") is a small, quiet game. A drawn tapir lives in a room on your phone. You pack it a bag and close the app; some time later it leaves on its own, sends a few short moments back from the road, and comes home with one postcard of a real place, which you can keep in an album. This page explains what data the app handles and who it is shared with. The app is provided by yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore ("we", "us").

Summary

  • There is no account and no sign-in. We do not know who you are, and we never ask for your name, your email or your phone number.
  • Your album, your settings and your trips are stored on your phone, in the app's own private storage. We never receive them. When you save a backup file or save a card as a picture, you choose where it goes and it goes only there.
  • The app requests no location permission of any kind and cannot read your GPS position. The places on the postcards are fixed artwork and text that ship inside the app; they have nothing to do with where you are. However, the analytics service described below does infer an approximate (country or city level) region from the IP address your requests come from, so this policy discloses approximate location as collected rather than claiming the app has none.
  • The app does send two things over the internet by default: a fixed, closed set of usage events (Google Analytics for Firebase) and crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics). Both can be switched off, and the first screen you ever see in the app tells you about them. Turning them off does not change the game in any way.
  • There are no ads and no advertising SDK, and the app does not collect an advertising ID: the three permissions that would let it read one are removed from the app package.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with anyone for advertising or for anyone else's own purposes.
  • The app is free. There is one optional one-time purchase handled entirely by Google Play. We never see or store your payment details.

What is stored on your phone

All of the following lives in the app's private storage on your device, and is never sent to us:

  • Your album — the postcards you have kept, the places they came from, their words, the date each first arrived and how many times you have been there.
  • Your journeys — the trips that have finished and the short moments that came back from the road during them.
  • The trip in progress — what you packed and when it is due home.
  • Your settings — the language, whether the pet may knock when it comes home, your quiet hours, and your two privacy switches.
  • Your one-time unlock, if you bought it — recorded as a simple yes or no, and re-checked with Google Play.

Uninstalling the app deletes all of it. You can also write an album backup to a file you choose, and read one back in; that file is yours, we never see it, and it contains album records only — not the pictures, not your settings and not a purchase.

What the app collects and sends

Two Google services run inside the app. Both are on when you first install it, both are described on the "Before you start" card the app shows on its very first run, and either can be turned off at any time in Settings → What is collected.

Usage analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase)

A fixed, closed set of events about how the app is used, so we can tell which parts people actually reach:

  • that a trip was prepared, that it departed, that it was notified home, and that a notification was opened;
  • that a postcard was opened, kept or saved as a picture;
  • that the album was opened, and that a card in it was reopened;
  • that a backup was exported;
  • that the one-time unlock was purchased;
  • how long the first screen was open and whether you touched the animal on it — as coarse ranges, never a time of day and never an exact count;
  • which of the app's own screens you reached;
  • how you say you heard about the app, if you answer that optional question;
  • plus the events Google Analytics collects automatically — first_open, session_start, user_engagement, screen_view, app_update, os_update, app_remove and app_exception — and standard device and diagnostic information such as device model, manufacturer, operating system version, app version and language.

Every value is a number, a yes/no, or a label from a fixed list. There is no free-text field anywhere in this reporting: the app provides no way for a place name, a postcard's words, a backup's contents or anything you typed to be recorded in it.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics)

If the app stops or misbehaves, a report is sent containing the technical details of the failure — the stack trace, the exception type, the moment it happened, and the state of the device at the time (model, operating system version, memory and storage available). Crash collection is switched off in the app package itself and only turned on once your choice has been read, so nothing is collected from somebody who switches it off on the first screen.

The identifiers this is tied to

The two services above tie what they send to installation identifiers generated by Google's own software: a Firebase installation ID, a Crashlytics installation UUID and an Analytics app-instance ID. They identify this installation of the app, not you. They are not derived from your device's hardware, from any account, or from an advertising ID — the app collects no advertising ID at all. Because these identifiers are stable for as long as the app is installed, we describe this reporting as pseudonymous rather than calling it anonymous.

Approximate location

The app asks for no location permission and cannot read your device's position. Google Analytics for Firebase nonetheless derives an approximate region — country, and sometimes city — from the IP address your requests arrive from, in the ordinary way any internet service sees an IP address. We use it only to know roughly where players are. Turning off usage analytics stops this too. Precise location is never collected.

Notifications

The app posts at most one notification a day, and only one kind: a quiet note that your pet has come home. Android asks for notification permission at the moment you first send the pet off, never before, and saying no does not stop the trip — the letter simply waits for you on the first screen. You can turn the knock off, and set quiet hours, in Settings. Notifications are scheduled and delivered entirely on your device; nothing about them is sent anywhere.

Purchases

The app is free and the album is never behind a paywall. There is a single optional one-time purchase that unlocks extra places for the pet to visit. It is sold through Google Play Billing: Google handles the payment and we never receive or store your card number, your billing address, your name or any other payment detail. What the app learns from Google Play is whether this Google account owns the unlock. Reinstalling the app, or installing it on another phone with the same Google account, restores the unlock from Google Play. A card you have already kept is never taken away, hidden or locked, whatever happens to a purchase.

Advertising

The app contains no advertising, no advertising SDK and no ad network. It does not collect an advertising ID: the AD_ID permission and both Privacy Sandbox advertising permissions are explicitly removed from the app package, and the analytics library is configured not to attach an advertising ID to anything it sends.

Who the data is shared with

The only recipients are the Google services the app is built on, which process the data described above on our behalf and under their own privacy policies:

  • Google Analytics for Firebase — the usage events, device and diagnostic information, and the IP-derived approximate region described above.
  • Firebase Crashlytics — crash reports.
  • Google Play Billing — the one-time purchase.

Google's handling of this data is governed by the Firebase privacy documentation and the Google Privacy Policy. There is no other third-party SDK in the app, no server of our own, and no analytics, attribution, advertising or social network beyond the three above. We do not sell your data and we do not share it with anyone for their own purposes or for advertising.

Keeping it, and getting rid of it

  • Turn it off: Settings → What is collected has a switch for usage analytics and a switch for crash reports. Turning either off stops that collection from that moment.
  • Uninstall: uninstalling the app stops all collection and deletes everything the app stored on your phone, including your album.
  • There is no account, so there is nothing for us to look up and nothing for you to log into. Because what is sent is tied only to a Google-generated installation identifier, we have no way to find "your" records — and no way to connect them to you. Crash reports are retained by Crashlytics for up to 90 days; Analytics data is retained for the period configured in Firebase and is deleted after that.
  • If you would like the collection stopped and you do not want to uninstall, the two switches above do exactly that. If you have any other request, write to yzyapps99@gmail.com and we will do what we can.

Children

This app is not directed to children. It is intended for teenagers and adults (13 and over), it is not part of Google Play's Designed for Families programme, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. If you believe a child has provided us with information, write to the address above.

Security

Everything the app sends travels over an encrypted connection. Everything the app keeps is in the app's own private storage, which other apps on the phone cannot read. We hold no server and no database of our own, so there is no account of yours for anyone to break into.

Changes to this policy

If what the app collects changes, this page changes with it and the date at the top is updated. A change that widens collection will also be described inside the app.

Contact

Questions, corrections or requests: yzyapps99@gmail.com (yzyy, Singapore).
