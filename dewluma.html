Dewluma: Glow Pet & Habits ("the app") is a small pet and habit app: you look after a glowing dew-lamp moth by doing small daily habits, and it brings back postcards and stories. This page explains every way data can leave your device, and what deleting your data does and does not delete. The app is provided by yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore ("we", "us", "the developer").

Summary

  • Your habits, notes and moods stay on your device; only the fixed usage values listed under Analytics are sent (for example your pet's stage, the personality trait of a postcard answer, and daily counts of completions), never names, notes, which habit or your mood. The developer has no server that receives your data.
  • Apart from those values, what you put in the app can leave your device only through Android backup, device-to-device transfer, or an export you start yourself (see When that data leaves your device).
  • The app sends fixed usage events to Google Analytics for Firebase and crash reports to Firebase Crashlytics. They never contain which habit you did, how you felt, or anything you wrote (see Analytics and Crash reports).
  • The app may download new postcard stories as static files from our content host, Cloudflare (see Story downloads).
  • Optional decor packs are one-time purchases through Google Play Billing.
  • No ads, no advertising ID, no accounts, no sign-in.

What you put in the app

Your habits, check-ins, mood ratings and notes, your pet's name and progress, postcards and decorations are stored on your device. They are not sent to the developer, and the developer has no server that receives them. This also covers the name you may give yourself, the answers you pick on postcards and journeys, and your reminder time. The only exception is the fixed values listed under Analytics, for example your pet's stage, the personality trait of a postcard answer you picked, and daily counts of completions. Names, notes, which habit you did and your mood are never sent.

Personalised lines

Some of your pet's lines mention your pet's name, your name, your habit names, or when you usually check in. They are put together on your device from what you've already entered. Nothing is sent anywhere, and the app keeps no extra record of it.

When that data leaves your device

Apart from the fixed Analytics values, there are three ways the data above can leave your device:

  1. Android backup: if backup is on in your phone's settings, Android includes this data in your Google account backup and may restore it on a new phone. Google stores and encrypts that backup; the developer cannot read it. You can turn backup off in system settings.
  2. Device-to-device transfer: when you move to a new phone, Android may copy the same data to it.
  3. Export: if you tap Export my data, the app creates a file and hands it to the app you choose (for example email or a file manager). What happens to it next is up to you and that app.

The backup and the transfer include your progress, your settings and the record of which story files the app has accepted. They never include your purchase records (see Purchases) or downloaded story files; pictures for stories restored from a backup are downloaded again.

Story downloads

The app may download new postcard stories (text and pictures) as static files from our content host, Cloudflare. The app never sends your habits, moods, your pet's name, your answers or your collection to the content host. Like any web request, the host processes technical information such as your IP address, the time and the file requested, to deliver the files and keep the service secure. When the app restores pictures for stories you already have, the files it requests show which story packs you have. Downloaded stories are kept on your device; Delete all data removes your stories and answers but keeps the shared story files, because they contain nothing about you. Pictures for stories restored from a backup are downloaded again and depend on our host being available.

These requests carry no account, advertising, analytics or install identifier and no cookies; the app sends a fixed "Dewluma" User-Agent instead of your device model. We do not turn on access log exports or web analytics for the content host. Cloudflare's own handling is described in the Cloudflare Privacy Policy.

Analytics (Google Analytics for Firebase, Google)

Usage events never include which habit you completed, how you felt, or anything you wrote. Habit and check-in completions are sent only as a count for each day, the next day you open the app, not when you complete them (apart from a one-time setup step during onboarding). Like any app analytics, they do show when the app is in use.

The app sends only these 16 events. Each one carries at most the fixed values listed; there is no free text:

  • app_open (no values)
  • onboarding_step: step (meet, name, owner, question, plan, first_action)
  • meet_result, sent once, with the next day's batch, after you first meet your pet: how the meeting began (hello, touch, meet_now, continue), how it ended (watched, meet_now, reduced_motion, fallback), whether it resumed after the app was closed (no, yes), and a time bucket in seconds (0_10, 10_20, 20_40, 40_90, 90_plus)
  • first_action (no values)
  • action_completed (no values; a count only)
  • pet_interaction: interaction kind (tap, long_press)
  • evolved: stage (larva, cocoon, moth)
  • outing_started: stage (larva, cocoon, moth)
  • outing_reward_granted: stage (larva, cocoon, moth)
  • postcard_opened (no values)
  • decor_pack_viewed: pack id (decor_moonlit_pond, decor_lantern_market, decor_bundle_two)
  • purchase_result: pack id, and purchase result (granted, pending, cancelled, failed, already_owned)
  • restore_result: restore result (restored, nothing_to_restore, failed)
  • welcome_back: days-away bucket (1 = 3 to 6 days, 2 = 7 to 29 days, 3 = 30 days or more)
  • postcard_choice: personality trait of the answer you picked (curious, gentle, brave, dreamy)
  • content_pack_result: content pack result (installed, network, signature, checksum, too_large, archive, schema, text, image, storage)

The app also sets one user property, install_source, to "play" or "other" (whether the app was installed from Google Play).

The SDK also sends an app-instance ID and device and session data (model, OS version, country derived from IP, app version, first-open and engagement time), and its own standard events such as first open, session start, app updates and in-app purchases. No advertising ID is collected, and no habit names, notes, mood ratings or pet names are sent. Advertising and ad-personalisation features are switched off. Data is sent encrypted (HTTPS). Google processes it for us as a service provider under the Google Privacy Policy; see also how Google uses data from apps that use its services.

Event-level data is kept for 2 months, then deleted by Google Analytics. Aggregated reports may be kept longer.

Crash reports (Firebase Crashlytics, Google)

If the app crashes, Firebase Crashlytics sends a crash report: stack traces, device model, OS version, app version and the time of a crash, with a random Crashlytics installation ID. They contain no habit content. Crashlytics keeps crash data for 90 days.

Purchases (Google Play Billing)

Google processes payments. The app stores the purchase token and product on this device, in a separate file that is not backed up, to know which decor packs you own. Purchases belong to your Google account and can be restored after reinstalling. Purchases are kept by Google Play, and you can restore them in Settings at any time. We never receive your card number, billing address or any other payment detail.

Deleting your data

Delete all data (Settings) removes your pet, habits, history, postcards, settings and reminders from this device, including your check-ins, moods, notes, answers and decorations, and the usage counts waiting to be sent. Your Play purchase record and the shared story files are kept so you can restore purchases and keep reading. It also resets the Google Analytics app-instance ID, so new usage events start under a new ID. It does not change other identifiers on the device (such as the Firebase installation ID or the Crashlytics installation ID), so it does not give the app a new device identity.

It does not delete data already sent to Google Analytics or Crashlytics; that expires on the schedule above. It does not delete an Android backup that was already made; a new backup replaces it. If you uninstall and reinstall the app before Android makes its next backup, Android may restore the data from before the reset. You can also delete backups in your Google account settings. It does not cancel or delete Play purchases: they are kept by Google Play and can be restored.

Because the app has no accounts, the developer cannot connect Analytics or crash data to you. If you want help, write to yzyapps99@gmail.com. Uninstalling the app deletes everything it stored on the device.

Permissions

The app requests: notifications (the one optional daily reminder), run at startup (to restore that reminder after the phone restarts), internet and network state (story downloads, Analytics, Crashlytics and Google Play), Google Play billing (purchases), wake lock (background tasks scheduled by Android's WorkManager) and the Google Play install referrer service (used by Google Analytics). It does not request location, camera, microphone, contacts, photos, or the advertising ID.

No ads, no accounts, not directed at children

The app shows no ads and has no accounts or sign-in; we never ask for your email or phone number. The app is not directed at children and is not part of Google Play's Designed for Families programme. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Who the data is shared with

We do not sell data and do not share it with anyone for their own use. Google processes the Analytics and Crashlytics data, and Google Play the purchases, on our behalf as described above; Cloudflare may deliver the story files.
