Privacy Policy — V-Taper: Physique Score

Last updated: August 23, 2026

Which app and developer this policy covers

This privacy policy applies to the following Android application and its developer, as listed on Google Play:

  • App name: V-Taper: Physique Score
  • Package name / application ID: com.yzy.vtaper
  • Google Play listing: play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.yzy.vtaper
  • Developer name (Google Play developer account): yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore
  • Contact email: yzyapps99@gmail.com

V-Taper ("the app") takes one photograph of you, measures the visible proportions of your body from it, and reports a score with one thing to work on. This page explains what data the app handles and who it is shared with. The app is provided by yzyy, an individual developer based in Singapore ("we", "us").

V-Taper is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, treat or prevent any condition, and it makes no body-fat, health or fitness claim.

Summary

  • Your photo never leaves your phone. The measurement runs entirely on your device. The photo is not uploaded, not sent to a server, not sent to any third party, and not used to train anything. We operate no servers of our own and there is nothing for us to look at.
  • No account is needed and the app never asks you for personal details. There is no sign-up, no login and no profile.
  • The only sensitive permission the app requests is the camera, and it is used for nothing but taking the scan you asked for.
  • There are no ads, no advertising SDK, and the app cannot collect an advertising ID.
  • The app does send crash reports and counts of what happened, so it can be fixed and improved. These are tied to a random installation identifier, so they are not anonymous, and they never include your photo, your score or any measurement. You can switch this off in the app's About screen.

Your photos and your scores

When you take a scan, the photo is analysed on your device. The app uses on-device machine-learning models that are built into the app itself, so no part of the picture is sent anywhere to be processed, and the measurement works with no network connection at all.

The photo and the numbers taken from it are written to the app's own private storage on your phone, where no other app can read them. They are the only copy that exists. They are also excluded from Android's cloud backup and from Android's device-to-device transfer, deliberately: a photograph of your body must not travel through a backup, so it stays on the phone that took it and does not follow you to a new one.

Your photos and scores are never sent to us, never included in crash reports or usage counts, and never shared with any third party. The app has no code that could send them.

Deleting your scans

Delete my scans on the app's Home screen erases every photograph and every score on the phone, permanently. There is no other copy anywhere — nothing was ever uploaded — so there is nothing left to delete once you have done it, and nothing for you to ask us to delete on your behalf.

One thing deliberately survives: a single number recording how many scans this phone has completed. It exists only so that deleting your history does not hand back the free first scan, it contains no measurement and nothing that identifies you, and it is removed when you uninstall the app.

Uninstalling the app removes everything it stored, including that number.

Crash reports and usage counts

Unless you switch it off, the app sends:

  • Crash reports — the technical details of a failure, such as the stack trace and the state of the device at the time.
  • Usage counts — that a scan was started; that it finished, or that the app asked for a retake and which of its own reasons applied; that the Pro screen was opened; that a purchase was started or completed. These are counts and fixed labels. No score, no ratio, no measurement and no photo is ever included, and the app provides no way for one to be recorded.

This information is tied to a random installation identifier generated by Google's software. We do not ask for or store anything that identifies you personally, but because that identifier is consistent for your installation, this reporting is pseudonymous rather than anonymous. We describe it plainly rather than calling it anonymous.

To turn it off: open the app, tap About and privacy on the Home screen, and switch off "Send crash reports and usage counts". Turning it off stops collection immediately, for both crash reports and usage counts, and the choice is remembered.

Network use

The app needs network access for two things only: sending the crash reports and usage counts described above, and confirming a purchase with Google Play. Your photo is never sent over the network. Taking and scoring a scan works fully offline.

Purchases

The one-time Pro unlock is sold through Google Play. Google processes the payment and we never receive or store your payment details. Whether you own the unlock is stored on your device and re-checked with Google Play, so it can be restored if you reinstall the app. There is no subscription and no other charge.

Advertising

The app contains no advertising and no advertising SDK. The Android advertising-ID permissions are explicitly removed from the app, so it cannot collect an advertising ID even indirectly through the analytics software it includes, and the analytics software is separately configured not to attach one to what it sends.

Third-party services

The app includes the following Google services, which handle data under Google's own privacy policy:

  • Firebase Crashlytics — crash reports (stack traces, device state at the time of a crash).
  • Google Analytics for Firebase — the usage counts described above, plus standard device information such as device model and operating system version. Like any internet service, it also sees the IP address your device connects from, and Google derives an approximate location (typically city or region level) from it. The app itself has no location permission and cannot read your actual location.
  • Google Play Billing — the one-time Pro purchase.
  • Google ML Kit — the pose and silhouette models used to measure the photo. These run on your device, from models built into the app; the photo is not sent to Google or anywhere else to be analysed.

See Google's Privacy Policy for how Google handles this data.

Data retention and deletion

We do not hold any of your data ourselves, so there is nothing for us to delete on request. Data collected by the Google services above is retained under Google's retention policies: usage data is kept for a limited period, and crash reports only as long as they are useful for fixing problems. Switching off reporting in the About screen stops further collection, and uninstalling the app stops all collection.

Your photos and scores are yours and stay on your device, so deleting them is the "Delete my scans" control described above, or uninstalling the app.

Children

The app is a general-audience fitness tool and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children.

Changes

If our data practices change, we will update this page and the "Last updated" date above before the change takes effect.
